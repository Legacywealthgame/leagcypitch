You Graduated And Your Student Loans Came Due

The Card

Your student loans came due after graduation, and you had to adjust your budget to handle the payments. Planning ahead would have made the transition smoother.

The Fact

The average federal student loan balance is roughly $40,000, and monthly payments commonly fall in the $200 to $300 range depending on the repayment plan chosen, a real, recurring expense that many graduates don't fully budget for until the first payment is actually due.

Why This Matters

The loan doesn't come due as a surprise, the terms are known well before graduation. The transition is smoother for graduates who build the eventual payment into their budget in advance rather than discovering the real number for the first time when the first bill arrives.

A Real Example

A graduate spends his first months out of school budgeting as though his full paycheck were available, then is caught off guard when loan payments begin and immediately strain a budget built without them. A friend graduating with a similar loan balance had been mentally budgeting for the eventual payment throughout her final year of school, and the transition barely registers as a change once the first bill arrives.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

A known future expense should be budgeted for before it starts, not discovered the month it does. The loan terms are public well before the first payment is ever due.
