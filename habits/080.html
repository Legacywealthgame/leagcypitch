Bad Credit Cost You On Your Mortgage Rate

The Card

Your credit history was not strong when you applied for a mortgage, and it cost you a higher interest rate. The same home ended up costing significantly more.

The Fact

On a $300,000 30-year mortgage, the difference between a 6.5% rate and an 8% rate is roughly $305 a month and about $110,000 in total interest across the loan, for the exact same house.

Why This Matters

Credit isn't checked only when applying for something, it's being priced into every borrowing decision quietly, years before the application happens. By the time the mortgage application is filed, the score is already locked in, there's no negotiating it in the underwriting meeting itself.

A Real Example

A buyer with a spotty payment history qualifies for a mortgage at 8% instead of the 6.5% a stronger credit profile would have earned. Over thirty years, that gap costs roughly $110,000 more for the identical house a neighbor with better credit purchased the same month. The cost wasn't decided at the closing table, it was decided by years of payment history that led up to it.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Credit is priced into your life years before you apply for anything. Build it long before you need it, because there's no fixing it in the meeting where it actually gets used.
