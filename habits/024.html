You Looked Wealthy Instead Of Building Wealth

The Card

You financed the car, the watch, and the lifestyle while building very few assets. Looking successful and being financially secure are not the same thing.

The Fact

A financed luxury vehicle typically loses a significant share of its value within the first few years while the loan balance remains largely unchanged, meaning the owner can owe more than the car is worth for a meaningful stretch of the loan.

Why This Matters

Visible wealth and actual wealth are frequently inversely related, since the spending required to look affluent is usually the same spending that would otherwise have funded actual assets. Nobody's balance sheet is visible from the outside, only the spending is, which is exactly why the two get confused.

A Real Example

Two professionals earn similar incomes. One finances a luxury car, a watch, and a larger apartment, all visible signals of success, and has almost no savings. The other drives a modest, paid-off car and lives well below her means while investing the difference. From the outside, the first looks far more successful. On paper, the second has multiples more in actual net worth.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Nobody's net worth is visible in a parking lot. The people who look the wealthiest are frequently the ones with the least of it, because looking wealthy and being wealthy draw from the same paycheck.
