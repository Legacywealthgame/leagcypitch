You Got Married And Built Wealth Together

The Card

You chose a spouse who shared your financial values and vision for the future. Together, you built wealth as a team instead of two people managing money separately.

The Fact

A 2012 Kansas State University study, published in the journal Family Relations and drawing on more than 4,500 couples, found that disagreement about money early in a relationship was the strongest predictor of divorce, ahead of income, debt, or net worth.

Why This Matters

Two incomes managed as one household plan compound faster than two incomes managed as two separate agendas, and it isn't just about the math. Aligned financial values remove an entire category of conflict that otherwise resurfaces at every major decision, a house, a career change, how to raise kids.

A Real Example

Two households earn similar combined incomes. In one, both partners save and invest with a shared plan and a joint understanding of their goals. In the other, one partner saves aggressively while the other spends freely, with no shared agreement either way. Ten years later, the aligned household has built substantial net worth. The other has argued about money regularly and built almost nothing, despite comparable income.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Compatible values compound. Mismatched ones cancel each other out, no matter how much either partner individually earns.
