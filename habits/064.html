You Let Inflation Erode Your Money

The Card

You kept your money in places that did not keep up with inflation. Over time, what your money could buy quietly shrank, even though the balance stayed the same.

The Fact

At roughly 3% average annual inflation, $150,000 held in cash for 20 years buys about what $83,000 buys today, even though the account balance never dropped by a single dollar.

Why This Matters

Inflation doesn't show up as a withdrawal or a loss on a statement, which is exactly what makes it so easy to ignore. The number stays the same while what it can actually buy quietly shrinks every year, and the loss is only visible in hindsight, once the purchasing power is already gone.

A Real Example

A woman keeps a substantial sum in a low-interest savings account for two decades, feeling responsible because the balance never drops. When she finally goes to use it, she's startled by how much less it actually buys compared to when she first set it aside. Nothing was ever technically lost. It just quietly bought less every single year she let it sit still.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

A balance that never grows is still moving, just backward. Standing still with money is not actually standing still.
