You Earned Big Commissions And Failed To Plan For Taxes

The Card

You earned large commissions but did not set aside money for the taxes owed on them. When tax season arrived, the bill created a serious financial strain.

The Fact

Commission income, like most self-employed or 1099 income, generally has no automatic tax withholding the way a salaried paycheck does, meaning the obligation accumulates silently across the year and arrives in full unless deliberately set aside in advance.

Why This Matters

Large, irregular income feels like a windfall in the moment it's earned, and it's tempting to treat the full amount as spendable. A meaningful share of it is already owed the moment it's earned, whether or not it's set aside, and spending as though it isn't just delays the reckoning.

A Real Example

A salesperson has a strong year with substantial commission income and spends most of it, assuming taxes will "work out" the way they did at previous salaried jobs. Tax season arrives with a bill far larger than anticipated and no funds set aside to cover it, forcing a payment plan with the IRS. The following year, she sets aside a percentage of every commission check into a separate account the moment it arrives, and the same size tax bill is a non-event.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Treat every dollar of irregular income as partially spoken for the moment it lands. The tax is owed regardless of whether you planned for it.
