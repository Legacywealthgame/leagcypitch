You Invested Your NIL Income

The Card

You earned money as a college athlete and chose to invest instead of spending it all. You learned how compound growth works and gave your money more time to build your future.

The Fact

$15,000 invested at 18 and left to grow at an 8% average return reaches roughly $82,000 by age 40, without another dollar ever being added.

Why This Matters

NIL income is a genuinely new opportunity, and how it's handled in these first few years sets a pattern that either becomes a lifelong habit or a lifelong regret. Money earned this early has more runway to compound than almost any income a person will ever receive again.

A Real Example

A college athlete earns NIL income and invests a meaningful portion of it instead of spending all of it on lifestyle upgrades while still in school. Two decades later, that early investment has grown into a substantial sum, built entirely from money earned before he ever had a full-time job, simply because it was given decades instead of years to compound.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

The earliest money you ever earn has the most time to grow. What you do with it matters more than what you do with money earned twenty years later.
