You Bought A Home Without Running The Numbers

The Card

You bought a home based on what felt right instead of running the actual numbers. The true cost of ownership turned out to be more than you expected.

The Fact

A common rule of thumb puts the true annual cost of owning a home, taxes, maintenance, insurance, and the opportunity cost of the money tied up in it, at roughly 5% of the home's value per year, well beyond the mortgage payment alone.

Why This Matters

Comparing only the mortgage payment to what feels affordable leaves out a substantial share of the real cost of ownership. Buying without running the full numbers means discovering that gap after closing, when the options for adjusting are far more limited.

A Real Example

A couple buys a $400,000 home based on the mortgage payment feeling comfortable next to their income, without factoring in taxes, insurance, and a realistic maintenance reserve. Once those costs are included, their true monthly housing cost runs several hundred dollars higher than they'd planned for, straining a budget that had no room built in for the difference.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

The mortgage payment is not the cost of the home, it's a piece of it. Run the full number before signing, not after.
