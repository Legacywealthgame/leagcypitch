You Had No Cushion When Your Car Broke Down

The Card

Your car broke down and you had no savings set aside to cover it. What should have been a manageable repair became a financial emergency.

The Fact

A car repair in the $1,000 to $2,000 range is common enough that it should be treated as a predictable expense, not a rare emergency, yet without dedicated savings it frequently gets funded through high-interest debt instead.

Why This Matters

The size of an expense matters less than whether it was anticipated. A $1,500 repair is a minor inconvenience with savings in place and a real financial setback without them, since the same bill that gets paid in cash in one scenario gets financed at 22% interest in the other.

A Real Example

Two coworkers both experience a $1,400 car repair the same month. One pays it from a dedicated savings buffer and moves on without a second thought. The other puts it on a credit card, and between the interest and the minimum payments, it takes over a year to fully pay off, turning a one-time bill into an ongoing cost.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Predictable-but-irregular expenses, car repairs, appliance failures, aren't emergencies if you've planned for them. They only become emergencies in the absence of a plan.
