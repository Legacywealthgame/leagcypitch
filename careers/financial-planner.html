The Real Path

Becoming a financial planner usually starts with a bachelor’s degree in finance or a related field, followed by the Certified Financial Planner certification. The CFP requires specific coursework, a rigorous exam, and thousands of hours of real experience, but unlike medicine or law it doesn’t demand a separate multi year graduate program or six figures of new debt.

Early in the career, income looks modest. Financial planners with less than five years of experience earn a median of around $107,500. But the trajectory changes once the CFP is earned and client relationships build. The median CFP professional earns around $185,000, and later career planners with two decades of experience and a real book of clients report medians north of $350,000. Income tends to climb steadily with the size and loyalty of the client base a planner builds over time.

That’s the financial planner at face value. Real trust based work, strong mid to late career income, but the client relationships and the revenue they produce still belong to the firm employing the planner, not the planner.

The Optimization

The optimization is owning the firm those clients belong to. A planner who leaves to start their own Registered Investment Advisor practice keeps the entire fee stream from every client relationship they build, instead of splitting it with an employer. And the ceiling on that is enormous. Independent advisory firms are bought and sold constantly, and firms with a few hundred million in assets under management routinely sell for several times their annual revenue. At real scale, RIA firms managing a billion dollars or more in client assets sell for double digit multiples of their earnings, sometimes into the tens or hundreds of millions of dollars in a single transaction. That’s not one advisor’s annual commission anymore, that’s the value of an entire client base and revenue stream built over a career, cashed out in one exit. Same trust, same fiduciary relationship with clients, but now the income comes from owning the practice instead of being employed by one.
