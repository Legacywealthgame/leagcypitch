The Real Path

Becoming a real estate developer starts with buying land, not a finished property, and taking on the full risk of turning it into something. That means acquiring the site, usually 20 to 50 percent of total project cost depending on the location, then financing the design, permitting, and construction on top of it. This is a fundamentally different game than buying an existing rental. A developer is creating value out of raw land instead of purchasing value someone else already built.

Once a project is built and sold, the numbers can be strong. Residential development, whether that’s single family homes, townhouses, or luxury units, typically nets a profit margin somewhere between 15 and 30 percent of total project cost. But every one of those dollars is tied up for months or years while the project gets built, and the money doesn’t show up until the units are sold.

That’s the real estate developer at face value. Real value creation, real ownership from the ground up, but income comes in large, infrequent chunks tied to how many projects one person or company can finance and build at a time.

The Optimization

The optimization is exactly what happens when a developer stops building one project at a time and starts running multiple developments, multiple property types, and multiple markets simultaneously. Stephen Ross started Related Companies in 1972 with a $10,000 loan from his mother, building small scale affordable housing. Related now holds a real estate portfolio worth well over $70 billion, spanning luxury residential towers, mixed use projects, retail, and office space across major cities. Its single largest project, Hudson Yards in Manhattan, cost roughly $25 billion to build and created an entire neighborhood from what used to be a rail yard. Ross’s personal net worth is now estimated at around $17 billion, built from decades of stacking projects instead of building one house at a time. Same core skill, buying land and creating value on top of it, but at scale it becomes an empire spanning every kind of property, from workforce housing to the most expensive luxury towers in the country.
