Our Story

What Wealthy Families Already Know

Some families talk about money at the dinner table. They talk about ownership, about assets, about what compounds and what drains. Their children grow up fluent in a language most people never get taught.

Legacy Wealth was designed by a financial planner to put that conversation on every family's table. Not a lecture, and not a worksheet — a game your family actually wants to play, built on real careers, real assets and real businesses, where the lessons land because you lived them for an evening.

The Game

Play the Decisions,
Not the Dice

Take a career. Buy assets. Pitch businesses. Duel your family over what you actually know. Every lap around the board is a year of financial decisions compressed into a few minutes.

Pick your career

Start as an Employee, an Investor or a Business Owner. Each career sets your starting cash, your income per lap, your debt, how many assets you can hold, and one advantage nobody else has.

Collect on every lap

Pass GO and collect your salary and the income from every asset you own. Earned income is where you start. Asset income is how you win.

Buy real assets

Six decks of Real Estate, Business and Investment assets, each with a cost and the income it returns every lap — from Bitcoin to a Manhattan penthouse to a commercial tower.

Live with your habits

The most common space on the board. Rich Habits pay you. Poor Habits cost you. Every card explains exactly why, so the lesson outlives the turn.

Survive the downside

Market Crash. IRS Audit. Foreclosure. The board does what the real economy does, and the players who built durable income are the ones still standing.

Win on Legacy Value

Not cash. Legacy Value — what you built and what it produces. The first player to a million takes it.

Impact

What Your Family
Takes With Them

The game ends in an evening. What it teaches is meant to outlast it.

Money stops being taboo

Most families never talk about money because there's no comfortable way in. A board and a set of rules gives everyone permission, and the eight-year-old gets to ask the question the adults were avoiding.

Assets become obvious

After one game, the difference between something that pays you every lap and something that just costs you isn't a lecture. It's the thing that decided who won.

Habits get named

Lifestyle creep. Good debt and bad. Giving up on the first failure. Once a family has language for these, they can spot them in real life.

The next generation starts ahead

Children who grow up fluent in ownership, income and compounding make different decisions at twenty-two. That head start is the whole point.
