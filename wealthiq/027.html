What is the difference between a traditional IRA and a Roth IRA?

The Answer

IRA stands for Individual Retirement Account. With a Traditional IRA, taxes are generally paid later when money is withdrawn. With a Roth IRA, you pay taxes before contributing, and qualified withdrawals can be tax free.

The Fact

Both accounts grow without annual taxation along the way. The difference is timing. Traditional contributions may be deductible now, with withdrawals taxed later. Roth contributions are made with money already taxed, and qualified withdrawals come out tax free, including all the growth.

Why This Matters

The choice comes down to one prediction: whether your tax rate will be higher now or later. Roth also means the number on the statement is the number you actually have, while a traditional balance still has taxes owed against it. Two people with identical $500,000 balances can have meaningfully different amounts of spendable money.

A Real Example

Marcus and Elena both retire with $500,000. Marcus used a traditional IRA, so every withdrawal is taxable income. At a 22% effective rate, roughly $110,000 of his balance belongs to taxes. Elena used a Roth. Her $500,000 is $500,000. They saved identically for thirty years and Elena has substantially more usable money, decided by which account they chose at the start.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

A traditional balance is a number with a bill attached that has not arrived yet. A Roth balance is the actual number. Know which one you are looking at.
