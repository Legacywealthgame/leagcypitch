What is the difference between an asset and a liability?

The Answer

An asset puts money in your pocket or grows your wealth. A liability takes money out. Wealth builders focus on buying assets before adding more liabilities.

The Fact

The test is direction of cash flow. An asset sends money toward you or grows in value. A liability sends money away from you. The same category of item can land on either side depending on how it is used.

Why This Matters

Most people sort their finances by what things cost rather than by what they do. That is how someone ends up with an impressive list of possessions and a net worth that has not moved in a decade. Sorting by direction instead of by price is what makes the picture honest.

A Real Example

Two people each own a $45,000 vehicle. Deon uses his for commuting. It loses value every year, costs him insurance, fuel, and maintenance, and produces nothing. It is a liability. Marissa uses hers for a delivery contract that nets about $1,900 a month after every vehicle expense. The same category of item pays her. One purchase, two completely different roles.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Do not ask what something is worth. Ask which direction it moves money. That single question sorts almost everything correctly.
