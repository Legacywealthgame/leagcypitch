What are some expenses a business owner may be able to write off?

The Answer

Business expenses can include things like marketing, software, business mileage, phone and internet costs, and certain workspace expenses when they are used for the business.

The Fact

Deductible business expenses generally must be ordinary and necessary for the business. When an expense has both business and personal use, only the business portion is generally deductible, which is why documentation matters.

Why This Matters

This is the mechanism behind why business owners are taxed on profit rather than gross revenue. Owners who track expenses poorly are effectively choosing to be taxed on money they already spent operating. It is also an area where records matter as much as eligibility, since an undocumented deduction is difficult to support.

A Real Example

Camille runs a consulting business and tracks nothing for the first year, deducting only obvious costs. The following year she tracks properly: software subscriptions, business mileage, the business-use share of her phone and internet, professional development, and a qualifying home office. Her deductible expenses rise by roughly $11,000 on identical spending. Nothing about her business changed except the recordkeeping.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

The deduction exists whether or not you track it. Only the tracked version actually reaches your return. Work with a tax professional on the specifics for your situation.
