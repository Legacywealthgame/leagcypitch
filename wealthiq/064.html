Why is relying only on Social Security not a complete retirement plan?

The Answer

Social Security is meant to provide part of your retirement income, not necessarily everything you need. A stronger retirement plan also includes savings, investments, pensions when available, and other sources of income.

The Fact

Social Security was designed as a partial income replacement, replacing roughly 40% of pre-retirement income for a typical worker, with more for lower earners and less for higher earners. It was built to supplement other retirement income, not to replace a full working income.

Why This Matters

A 40% replacement rate means a substantial gap between the benefit and the cost of the life someone is used to. People planning to rely on it exclusively are typically planning around a number that has never been calculated, and the shortfall becomes visible only once working income has already stopped.

A Real Example

Yolanda earns $72,000 before retiring and expects Social Security to carry her. Her benefit comes in around $2,300 a month, roughly $27,600 a year. Her actual expenses, even after paying off her mortgage, run closer to $46,000. She is short about $18,000 every year, with no remaining way to earn it. The gap existed for decades before it became visible.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Calculate your expected benefit against your actual expenses now, while there is still time to close the gap. Most people first do that math the year they retire.
