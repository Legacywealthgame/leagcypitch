What does it mean to have a diversified investment portfolio?

The Answer

It means spreading your money across different investments instead of putting everything in one place. Diversification helps reduce the damage if one investment performs badly.

The Fact

Diversification reduces the impact of any single investment failing. It does not eliminate market risk, since a broad downturn affects most holdings, but it eliminates the risk of one company or one sector destroying an entire portfolio.

Why This Matters

The most severe investing losses usually come from concentration, not from market declines. Markets recover. Individual companies sometimes do not. Diversification is what ensures that being wrong about one thing is survivable rather than final.

A Real Example

Two employees at the same company each hold $150,000 in retirement savings. Ray holds all of it in company stock, which he understands and believes in. Nadia holds a diversified fund. The company hits serious trouble, the stock falls sharply, and layoffs follow. Ray loses a large share of his savings and his income in the same quarter. Nadia loses her job and her portfolio is largely intact.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Never hold your investments and your income in the same company. That is one risk wearing two costumes.
