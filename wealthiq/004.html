What is lifestyle creep, and why can it stop you from building wealth?

The Answer

Lifestyle creep is when your spending rises every time your income rises. Instead of investing the extra money, you spend more. You can make more money and still stay broke.

The Fact

A $10,000 annual raise, invested consistently at an 8% average return, grows to roughly $1.25 million over 30 years. Spent instead, it grows to nothing. The raise is identical. The outcome differs by more than a million dollars based on one decision made the month it arrived.

Why This Matters

Lifestyle creep is the reason high earners go broke and the reason a raise never seems to change anything. Spending rises to meet income almost automatically unless someone deliberately intervenes. The result is a person who earns three times what they did a decade ago, feels exactly as stretched, and cannot point to where the difference went.

A Real Example

Over eight years Andre goes from $58,000 to $126,000. Every raise arrives, and within about two months a new payment arrives with it: a bigger apartment, an upgraded car, a higher standard of everything. At $126,000 he has $4,000 saved. He more than doubled his income and did not change his financial position at all, because every additional dollar was already assigned before it landed.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

The raise is not the win. What the raise gets assigned to is the win. Decide before it arrives, because after it arrives the decision makes itself.
