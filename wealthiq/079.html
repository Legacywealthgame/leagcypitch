If your 401(k) charges a 3% annual fee for 35 years, about how much can those fees reduce your final retirement balance?

The Answer

More than 60%.

The Fact

$25,000 growing at 7% for 35 years becomes roughly $267,000. The same $25,000 growing at 4%, which is what a 3% annual fee leaves you, becomes roughly $99,000. That is about $168,000 gone, a reduction of roughly 63%, from a number most people never look up.

Why This Matters

A 3% fee does not sound alarming. It sounds like a rounding error next to a 7% return. But fees compound exactly the way returns do, just against you, and over a full working career that difference stops being a fee and becomes most of the account. People obsess over picking the right fund and never check what the plan charges to hold it.

A Real Example

Keisha rolls an old 401(k) into an IRA and finally reads the fee disclosure. Her old plan charged 2.9% all in between the fund expense ratios and plan administration. The index fund in her new IRA charges 0.04%. Same money, same market, same contributions going forward. That single afternoon of paperwork is worth more to her retirement than several years of additional contributions would have been.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Returns are uncertain every year. Fees are guaranteed every year. The guaranteed number deserves at least as much attention as the uncertain one.
