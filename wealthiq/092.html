What does Customer Lifetime Value mean in business?

The Answer

Customer Lifetime Value is how much money a customer is expected to spend with a business over the entire relationship. The longer customers stay and buy, the more valuable they become.

The Fact

Customer Lifetime Value is calculated from average purchase value, purchase frequency, and how long a customer typically stays. A customer spending $50 monthly for 24 months has a lifetime value of $1,200, not $50.

Why This Matters

Lifetime value determines how much a business can afford to spend acquiring a customer, which determines how aggressively it can grow. It also reframes retention as a growth strategy rather than a defensive one, since extending the average relationship raises the value of every customer the business will ever acquire.

A Real Example

A gym owner sees each member as $60 a month and hesitates to spend more than $60 acquiring one. When he calculates that the average member stays fourteen months, lifetime value is about $840. He can now justify spending $200 to acquire a member and still profit substantially. That single calculation unlocks a marketing budget he had been refusing himself.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Extending how long customers stay raises the value of every future customer at once. Retention is usually cheaper than acquisition and almost always neglected.
