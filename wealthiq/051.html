What is generational wealth?

The Answer

Generational wealth is money, assets, or resources passed down from one generation to the next. It can include investments, property, businesses, or savings. The goal is to give future generations a financial head start instead of starting from zero each time.

The Fact

Generational wealth requires two separate things: assets that outlast the owner, and a structure that transfers them without being consumed by court costs and delay. Assets without a transfer plan often lose a meaningful share of their value on the way down.

Why This Matters

Most families restart from zero every generation, not because nothing was earned, but because nothing was structured to survive the handoff. Income does not transfer. Skills transfer imperfectly. Assets held in the right structures transfer directly. The difference between a family that compounds across generations and one that resets is usually planning, not earning.

A Real Example

Two families each build roughly $700,000 over a working lifetime. The first holds it in a trust with current beneficiary designations and a documented plan for the family business. It transfers within weeks, largely intact. The second has no plan. Probate consumes a substantial share in fees, the process takes over a year, and the business loses most of its value while nobody has legal authority to run it.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Generational wealth is not just what you accumulate. It is what survives the transfer. Those are two different projects and most families only finish the first.
