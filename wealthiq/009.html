Why Compound Interest Is So Powerful

See It Clearly

Compound interest is a snowball rolling downhill: small and slow at first, but picking up size and speed the longer it's allowed to roll.

In the Real World

Warren Buffett built the overwhelming majority of his net worth after age 60, not because his strategy changed, but because decades of compounding on decades of prior gains finally caught up.

The Fact

Investing $300 a month starting at 25 and earning a 7% average annual return grows to roughly $787,000 by 65. Waiting until 35 to start the same $300 a month only reaches about $366,000, a difference of over $420,000 created entirely by ten years of lost time.

What the Wealthy Do

The wealthy start early and stay invested, understanding that time in the market does more heavy lifting than the size of any single contribution.
