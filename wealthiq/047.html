What a 401(k) Vesting Period Means

See It Clearly

Vesting is like a gift that only becomes fully yours after you've stuck around long enough, leave too soon and the giver can take part of it back.

In the Real World

An employee who leaves a job one month before their four-year vesting date can lose thousands of dollars in employer contributions that were sitting in their account the entire time.

The Fact

A vesting period is the length of time an employee must stay with an employer before fully owning the employer's matching contributions, and it can range anywhere from immediate to several years depending on the plan.

What the Wealthy Do

The wealthy factor vesting schedules directly into job-change decisions, since leaving too early can mean forfeiting real employer-funded money already sitting in the account.
