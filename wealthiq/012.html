Why should you know what fees you are paying on your investments?

The Answer

Because fees come out of your returns. Even a small fee can cost you a lot of money over time because that money never gets the chance to compound.

The Fact

Over 35 years, a 1% annual fee on a growing portfolio can consume a substantial share of the final balance, and a 3% fee can consume more than 60% of it. The fee is charged on the entire balance every year, including all the growth that fee has already prevented.

Why This Matters

Fees are expressed in decimals specifically because decimals sound harmless. One percent reads as nothing. But a fee is guaranteed, charged whether the market rises or falls, and it compounds against you for as long as the money exists. It is the only variable in investing that is certain, which makes it the one most worth controlling.

A Real Example

Deshawn compares two funds tracking the same index. One charges 0.04%, the other charges 1.1%. Both hold nearly identical companies and deliver nearly identical gross returns. On a $200,000 balance, the difference is roughly $2,120 a year in fees, every year, for identical performance. Over decades that gap is a house down payment, and the funds themselves are functionally the same product.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

You cannot control returns. You can control fees completely. Spend your attention on the one you actually decide.
