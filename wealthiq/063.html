Will vs. Living Trust

The Fact

A will must pass through probate, a public court process that takes time and costs money before assets are distributed. A living trust transfers assets directly to beneficiaries without probate, privately and typically faster.

What the Wealthy Do

Wealthy families frequently use living trusts specifically to avoid probate, protect privacy, and keep control over exactly how and when assets are distributed.

See It Clearly

A will is a letter left for a judge to interpret in public. A trust is a set of instructions that execute automatically, privately, without needing a courtroom at all.

In the Real World

A family with a living trust can typically settle an estate in weeks. A family relying only on a will can be tied up in probate court for months or years on an identical estate.
