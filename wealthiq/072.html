What is sequence of returns risk in retirement?

The Answer

It is the risk of the market dropping early in retirement while you are also taking money out. Selling investments during a major drop can make your retirement money run out faster.

The Fact

Two retirees can experience identical average returns over a full retirement and end with very different outcomes depending on the order those returns arrived. Poor returns early, combined with withdrawals, permanently reduce the base that later gains can work on.

Why This Matters

While you are saving, order does not matter much because you are adding rather than removing. In retirement, order becomes critical, since selling during a decline locks in losses on shares that never recover. The first several years of retirement carry outsized influence on whether the money lasts.

A Real Example

Two people retire with $1 million and withdraw $45,000 a year. Elena's first three years bring strong returns, so her balance grows even while she withdraws. Ray's first three years bring a sharp decline, forcing him to sell more shares at depressed prices to fund the same withdrawals. Even if their average returns match over thirty years, Ray's balance may never recover, purely because of when the bad years landed.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Hold one to two years of expenses in cash or stable assets entering retirement. That reserve is what lets you avoid selling into a decline during the years it does the most damage.
