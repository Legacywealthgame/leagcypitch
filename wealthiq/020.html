What is an index fund?

The Answer

An index fund lets you invest in many companies at once instead of putting all your money into one company. It is a simple way to spread your risk.

The Fact

A single S&P 500 index fund purchase gives you a position in roughly 500 large U.S. companies at once. If one company fails entirely, it is a fraction of the fund rather than the whole investment.

Why This Matters

Picking individual winners requires being right about a specific company's future, repeatedly, over decades. An index fund removes that requirement by owning the whole group. It also removes the single largest risk in stock investing, which is not the market falling, but having everything concentrated in one company that falls and never recovers.

A Real Example

Two coworkers each invest $20,000. Alicia puts it all into her employer's stock, which she knows well and believes in. Ben puts his into a broad index fund. Alicia's company hits a rough stretch and the stock drops 60%, which also happens to be the year layoffs come. Her investment and her income take the hit at once. Ben's fund holds hundreds of companies, several of which had a bad year, and his balance is roughly flat.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Concentration builds fortunes and destroys them. Diversification does neither dramatically, which is exactly why it works for money you cannot afford to be wrong about.
