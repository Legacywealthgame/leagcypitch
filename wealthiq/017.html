What is the difference between good debt and bad debt?

The Answer

Good debt helps you buy something that can make you money or grow in value. Bad debt costs you money without helping you build wealth.

The Fact

The distinction is not about the interest rate or the size of the loan. It is about what sits on the other side of it. Debt used to acquire something that produces income or appreciates has a chance of paying for itself. Debt used for consumption has no mechanism to ever do so.

Why This Matters

"All debt is bad" and "leverage is how the wealthy build" are both taught confidently, and both are incomplete. The useful question is not whether to borrow, it is what the borrowed money is buying. A mortgage on a rental that cash flows and a balance on a card for a vacation are treated as the same category by people who only sort debt by how much they owe.

A Real Example

Two people each take on $40,000 of debt in the same month. Priya borrows to buy a rental unit that nets about $400 a month after every expense including the loan. The debt pays its own cost and leaves her ahead. Reggie finances a $40,000 truck upgrade he did not need. It loses value from the first day and produces no income. Identical amount borrowed, opposite direction.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Before borrowing, ask what the money is buying and whether that thing can pay the loan back on its own. If the answer is no, the debt is being paid entirely out of your life.
