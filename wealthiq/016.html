If your mortgage payment is $3,000 a month, does all $3,000 reduce what you owe?

The Answer

No. Part goes toward the loan balance and part goes toward interest. Especially early in the loan, less of your payment may be building equity than you think.

The Fact

On a $300,000 mortgage at 7%, the monthly payment is about $1,996. In the very first month, roughly $1,750 goes to interest and only about $246 reduces the balance. Across the entire first year, about $20,900 goes to interest and only about $3,050 goes to principal.

Why This Matters

Most people believe they start building equity from day one at roughly the rate of their payment. Early on, the overwhelming majority of every payment is the cost of borrowing, not ownership. This is why someone can pay a mortgage faithfully for three years and be startled by how little the balance moved, and it is why extra principal payments early in a loan are dramatically more powerful than the same payments later.

A Real Example

The Ortegas pay about $1,996 a month for three years, roughly $71,800 total. They expect to have knocked a serious dent in a $300,000 loan. The balance has dropped by roughly $10,000. Nothing went wrong and no payment was missed. The loan is simply structured so interest is front-loaded, and nobody explained that at closing.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

A single extra principal payment in year one does far more than the same payment in year twenty, because it eliminates all the interest that dollar would have generated for the remaining term.
