Wealth IQ Card 044

Someone has contributed to a 401(k) for 40 years. What determines whether they actually have enough money to retire comfortably?

The Answer

How much they contributed, how their money grew, the fees they paid, and what other income they have in retirement, such as Social Security, a pension, savings, or personal investments. A 401(k) by itself does not guarantee a comfortable retirement.

The Fact

A widely used planning benchmark is 25 times your annual expenses, based on withdrawing roughly 4% a year. Someone who needs $80,000 a year needs roughly $2 million. Forty years of contributing tells you nothing about whether that number was reached. Duration is not an amount.

Why This Matters

"I contributed for 40 years" feels like a finish line, and it is one of the most common ways people arrive at retirement unprepared while believing they did everything right. Two people can both contribute for four decades and land in completely different places depending on how much went in, what it was invested in, and what the plan charged them along the way.

A Real Example

Two coworkers both retire after 40 years at the same company. Dale contributed 4% and never touched his allocation, sitting mostly in a conservative fund with a 1.2% expense ratio. Renee contributed 12%, captured the full employer match, and used a low-cost index fund. Same employer, same years, same salary band. Renee retires with several times what Dale has. Neither of them "did nothing." Only one of them tracked the actual number.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

A 401(k) is a container, not a plan. What matters is what you put in it, what it grew to, and what else is standing next to it.
