What does "minimum payment trap" mean?

The Answer

It happens when you keep paying only the minimum on credit card debt. The debt can take much longer to pay off, and interest can make you pay far more than you originally spent.

The Fact

A $6,000 balance at 22%, paying only the typical minimum, takes roughly 21 years to clear and costs close to $10,000 in interest. Paying a fixed $250 a month instead clears the same balance in under three years for about $2,000 in interest.

Why This Matters

The minimum payment is designed to keep the account current, not to eliminate the debt. Because it drops as the balance drops, the payoff timeline stretches almost indefinitely. Most people assume the minimum is a recommendation from the lender about what they should pay. It is the smallest amount that prevents default, which is a different thing entirely.

A Real Example

Tasha carries $6,000 and pays the minimum faithfully every month, never missing one. Four years in, she has paid thousands and her balance has barely moved, because most of each payment went to interest. When she switches to a fixed $250 a month and never lets it drop, the same debt disappears in under three years.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Never let the payment shrink with the balance. Pick a fixed number and hold it until the debt is gone.
