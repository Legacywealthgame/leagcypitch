What is the S&P 500?

The Answer

It is an index that tracks about 500 large U.S. companies. People often use it as a quick way to see how a big part of the stock market is performing.

The Fact

The S&P 500 tracks roughly 500 of the largest publicly traded U.S. companies and is weighted by market value, meaning larger companies influence the index more than smaller ones. When someone says "the market was up today," they are usually referring to this index.

Why This Matters

The S&P 500 functions as the default benchmark for U.S. investing. It is what fund performance gets measured against, what most retirement portfolios are partly built on, and what news reports mean when they describe the market's direction. Understanding it turns financial headlines from noise into information.

A Real Example

Jordan hears a fund advertised as returning 9% last year and it sounds strong. Then he checks the S&P 500 for that same year and it returned about 12%. The fund charged him a fee to underperform the benchmark he could have bought directly for almost nothing. Without knowing the benchmark existed, 9% sounded like good news.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Any return is meaningless without something to compare it to. The benchmark is what turns a number into a judgment.
