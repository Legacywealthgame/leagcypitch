What is your retirement number, and what is one simple way to estimate it?

The Answer

Your retirement number is how much money you may need invested to support your retirement. One common rule is to multiply the yearly amount you need by 25. If you need $80,000 a year, that would be about $2 million.

The Fact

The 25x guideline comes from the idea of withdrawing roughly 4% a year. Needing $60,000 a year points to about $1.5 million. $80,000 points to about $2 million. $100,000 points to about $2.5 million.

Why This Matters

Most people save toward retirement without ever calculating a target, which means they have no way to know whether they are ahead, behind, or close. A number turns retirement from a vague worry into a measurable gap you can actually work against. It also reveals early enough to matter whether the current contribution rate will get there.

A Real Example

Denise is 44 and has been contributing steadily for years without a target. She runs the math: her expenses will be roughly $70,000 a year, so her number is about $1.75 million. She has $310,000 saved. That gap is uncomfortable to see, and it prompts her to raise her contribution rate and capture her full employer match, twenty-one years before retirement rather than two.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Calculate the number even if it is intimidating. A gap you can see is a problem you can still solve.
