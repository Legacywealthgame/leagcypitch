How can compound interest work for you and how can it work against you?

The Answer

It works for you when investments keep growing on top of past growth. It works against you when debt keeps adding interest to what you owe. The same force can build wealth or destroy it.

The Fact

A $6,000 credit card balance at 22%, paying only the minimum, can take well over a decade to clear and cost more in interest than the original balance. The identical mechanism that turns $10,000 into $100,000 over thirty years turns a manageable balance into a multi-year obligation.

Why This Matters

Compounding does not care which direction it runs. Most financial education teaches it only as a wealth-building concept, so people understand it as something that helps them and never recognize it as the thing quietly working against them on a credit card statement. It is one force with two directions, and most households have it running both ways at once.

A Real Example

Simone has $12,000 invested growing at 8%, earning about $960 a year. She also carries a $9,000 credit card balance at 23%, costing about $2,070 a year. She thinks of herself as an investor building wealth. On net she is losing roughly $1,110 a year, because the same force is running harder against her than for her, on two statements she never looks at side by side.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Before asking what your money is earning, ask what your debt is charging. Those two numbers belong on the same page.
