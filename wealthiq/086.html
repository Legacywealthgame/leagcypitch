Wealth IQ Card 086

You have three debts. A car loan at 6 percent, student loans at 5 percent, and a credit card at 24 percent. You have $500 extra per month. What order should you pay it off in?

The Answer

Attack the credit card at 24 percent first. That is the most expensive debt and it compounds against you every month. Once it is eliminated, redirect that payment to the car loan. Then the student loans. This is the Avalanche Method.

The Fact

On a $5,000 balance at 24%, applying the extra $500 a month clears it in roughly 9 months for about $450 in interest. Send that $500 to the 6% car loan instead, and the card balance sits there compounding at 24% the entire time, costing several times more before it is gone.

Why This Matters

The Snowball Method, smallest balance first, gets taught constantly because early wins feel motivating. That is a real psychological benefit. But when the smallest balance is not the most expensive one, order costs you real money. Ranking by interest rate rather than balance size is the difference between paying off debt and paying for the privilege of paying off debt.

A Real Example

Carla has a $1,200 medical bill at 0%, a $5,000 credit card at 24%, and $18,000 in student loans at 5%. Snowball says kill the $1,200 first for the win. But that debt is charging her nothing. Every month she spends on it, the card is charging her about $100 in interest. She goes after the card instead. It takes longer to see the first balance hit zero, and it saves her over a thousand dollars.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Pay attention to the rate, not the size. The most expensive debt is rarely the one that feels the most urgent.
