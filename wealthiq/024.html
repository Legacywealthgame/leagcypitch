Is it usually cheaper to borrow money when interest rates are high or low?

The Answer

Low. Lower interest rates mean borrowing costs less and payments are usually smaller. Smart investors pay attention to the cost of money before using debt to buy assets.

The Fact

On a $300,000 30-year mortgage, a 4% rate produces a payment near $1,432 while a 7% rate produces a payment near $1,996. Same loan amount, same house, roughly $564 a month and more than $200,000 in additional interest across the term.

Why This Matters

Interest rates set the price of using other people's money, which determines what deals work and what deals do not. A rental property that produces solid returns at 4% financing can lose money at 7% on identical rent. Investors track rates for the same reason a retailer tracks wholesale cost, because it decides whether the transaction makes sense at all.

A Real Example

Devin runs the numbers on a duplex renting for $2,800 a month. At 4.5% financing, his mortgage, taxes, and insurance land near $2,300 and he clears about $500 a month. At 7.5%, the same purchase price puts him closer to $2,950, which means the property loses money every month. Same building, same rent, same tenant. The only thing that changed was the cost of the money.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Rates decide which deals exist. When money is expensive, fewer deals work, and the ones that do require better numbers going in.
