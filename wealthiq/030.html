What is an IUL (Indexed Universal Life) and what makes it different from a regular life insurance policy?

The Answer

IUL stands for Indexed Universal Life. It is permanent life insurance that builds cash value based on the performance of a market index while also providing life insurance protection.

The Fact

An IUL credits interest to its cash value based on the performance of a market index rather than by being directly invested in the market. Policies typically include a floor that limits losses in down years and a cap that limits gains in strong ones.

Why This Matters

This structure is what makes an IUL distinct from both term insurance and direct market investing. It is permanent coverage with a cash value component that participates in index performance within defined limits. Understanding the floor and cap is central to understanding what the policy actually does, since the tradeoff for downside protection is a ceiling on upside.

A Real Example

Nia holds an IUL with a 0% floor and a cap on annual crediting. In a year when the index falls sharply, her cash value is not credited a loss because of the floor. In a year when the index rises well above her cap, she is credited up to the cap rather than the full index gain. Both features are working exactly as the policy is designed, and both are the same tradeoff seen from opposite directions.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Every financial product trades something for something. With an IUL, the floor and the cap are two sides of one decision, and both belong in the conversation.
