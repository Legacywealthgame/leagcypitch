Before deciding whether to buy a home or keep renting, what numbers should you look at?

The Answer

Look at the mortgage, taxes, insurance, repairs, how long you plan to stay, and what else your down payment money could be doing. Buying is not automatically the better financial move.

The Fact

A common rule of thumb puts the true annual cost of owning, covering taxes, maintenance, insurance, and the opportunity cost of the money tied up in the home, at roughly 5% of the home's value per year. On a $400,000 home that is about $20,000 annually, or roughly $1,660 a month, separate from building any equity.

Why This Matters

Comparing a mortgage payment to a rent payment leaves out most of the actual cost of ownership, and it ignores what a large down payment could be earning somewhere else. Renting is not automatically wasted money, and buying is not automatically wealth building. The honest comparison includes every cost on both sides.

A Real Example

Nia is choosing between $2,100 rent and a $2,300 mortgage payment on a $400,000 home, and the mortgage looks close enough to be worth it. Then she adds property taxes, insurance, and a realistic maintenance reserve, and her true monthly cost is closer to $3,400. She also has $80,000 sitting in the down payment that would otherwise be invested. She decides to buy anyway, because she plans to stay twelve years. That is a sound decision, made with the real numbers instead of the comfortable ones.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Buy a home because you want that specific life, and know exactly what it costs. Problems start when people buy assuming it is automatically the better financial move and never check.
