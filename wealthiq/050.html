What is leverage in real estate, and why do investors use it?

The Answer

Leverage means using borrowed money to buy property instead of paying the full price yourself. It lets investors control a larger asset while keeping more of their own money available for other opportunities.

The Fact

With 20% down, $100,000 controls a $500,000 property. If that property appreciates 5%, the gain is $25,000, which is a 25% return on the $100,000 actually invested. The same appreciation on a $100,000 property bought outright would produce $5,000.

Why This Matters

Leverage multiplies returns on the money you put in, which is why real estate builds wealth faster than most assets for people who use it well. It also multiplies losses in the other direction, and it introduces a payment that must be made whether or not the property is occupied. Both effects come from the same mechanism.

A Real Example

Nia buys a $500,000 duplex with $100,000 down. Over five years the property appreciates to $580,000 and tenants pay down roughly $40,000 of principal. Her $100,000 has produced about $120,000 in combined equity gain. Buying a $100,000 property outright with the same cash would have produced a fraction of that, because appreciation applies to the full asset value, not to the amount she contributed.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Leverage amplifies whatever is already true about the deal. Good deal, bigger gain. Bad deal, faster damage. It is not a substitute for the deal being sound.
