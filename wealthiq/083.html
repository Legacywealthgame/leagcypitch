Your child just got their first paycheck and is earning their own money for the first time. What does a wealth building parent do with this moment?

The Answer

Turn it into a financial education moment by teaching them to save, invest, and spend intentionally.

The Fact

$50 a month invested from age 16 at an 8% average return grows to roughly $290,000 by age 65. The same $50 a month started at age 26 grows to roughly $128,000. Ten years of delay costs more than half the outcome, on identical contributions.

Why This Matters

A teenager's biggest financial asset is something no adult can buy back: time. Most parents either hand over full control, which teaches nothing, or take the money and save it themselves, which also teaches nothing. The window where the amounts are small enough that mistakes are cheap is exactly the window where the habit is worth building.

A Real Example

Jasmine's son earns $340 from his first month at a grocery store. Instead of a lecture, she sits down with him and they split it three ways: $170 to spend however he wants, $100 into savings, $70 into a custodial brokerage account they open together that afternoon. He picks the fund. Six months later he checks the balance without being asked. That is the actual goal, not the $70.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

You are not managing a teenager's $340. You are installing the operating system they will run for the next fifty years.
