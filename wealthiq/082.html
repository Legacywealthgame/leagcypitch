Wealth IQ Card 082

You receive a $10,000 year end bonus. You have no high interest debt and a solid emergency fund already in place. What should you do with the $10,000?

The Answer

Deploy it into an income producing asset or investment portfolio.

The Fact

Cash in a standard checking account typically earns close to nothing, often under 1%. The two reasons to hold cash instead of investing it, paying off expensive debt and covering emergencies, are already handled in this scenario. Neither one is available as a justification anymore.

Why This Matters

Money sitting still is not neutral. Over ten years, $10,000 left in checking stays roughly $10,000 while the same amount invested at historical market averages could more than double. Doing nothing feels safe, but in this specific situation, where the safety net already exists, it is simply a decision to forgo growth.

A Real Example

Renata gets a $10,000 bonus in December and leaves it in checking to "decide later." By March it is $6,400 after a vacation, a car repair, and a series of small purchases she cannot fully account for. Her colleague moved his bonus into his brokerage account the week it landed. Ten years on, that single decision is the difference between a spent bonus and a five-figure position.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Every dollar should have an assignment before it arrives. Undirected money finds its own destination, and it is rarely one you would have chosen.
