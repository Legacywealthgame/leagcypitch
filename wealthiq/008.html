The Rule of 72, Explained

The Fact

The Rule of 72 is a widely used mental-math shortcut in finance: divide 72 by your annual return rate to estimate how many years it takes an investment to double — at 8% that's 9 years, at 12% it's 6.

What the Wealthy Do

The wealthy use quick mental tools like this to evaluate an investment's real long-term power in seconds, without needing a calculator or spreadsheet in the moment.

See It Clearly

It's a speedometer for your money — a fast way to see how quickly you're moving toward double, without doing the full math every time.

In the Real World

An investor comparing a 4% bond to an 8% stock fund can use the Rule of 72 instantly: 18 years to double versus 9 — a gap that reshapes a 30-year retirement plan on the spot.
