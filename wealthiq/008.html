What simple rule can help you estimate how long it takes your money to double?

The Answer

The Rule of 72. Divide 72 by your yearly return rate and that tells you how many years it takes to double your money. At 8% your money doubles in about 9 years. At 12 percent it doubles in 6.

The Fact

The rule is remarkably accurate for typical return rates. At 8%, the rule says 9 years and the precise math says 9.01. At 12%, the rule says 6 and the true answer is 6.12. At 6%, the rule says 12 and the true answer is 11.9.

Why This Matters

The Rule of 72 turns compounding from an abstraction into something you can calculate in your head in three seconds. It also makes fees and rate differences suddenly visible. The difference between a 6% return and an 8% return does not sound like much until you realize it is the difference between doubling every 12 years and doubling every 9.

A Real Example

Jamal is 30 with $50,000 invested and wants to know what happens if he adds nothing else. At 8%, it doubles roughly every 9 years: $100,000 at 39, $200,000 at 48, $400,000 at 57, $800,000 at 66. He did not need software or a planner for that. He needed one division problem and the willingness to look at the whole timeline.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Compounding is not slow. It is back-loaded. The last doubling adds more than every doubling before it combined, which is why leaving money alone is the hardest and most valuable part.
