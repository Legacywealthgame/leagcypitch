What is the difference between principal and interest on a loan?

The Answer

Principal is the money you borrowed. Interest is what the lender charges you for using their money.

The Fact

Only the principal portion of a payment reduces what you owe. The interest portion is the cost of borrowing and reduces nothing. Two payments of identical size can lower a balance by very different amounts depending on how they split.

Why This Matters

People track the payment and assume the balance is dropping proportionally. It is not. Understanding the split is what makes extra payments make sense, since money applied directly to principal skips the interest cost entirely and permanently shortens the loan. Without knowing the difference, an extra payment feels like generosity toward the bank rather than a specific financial move.

A Real Example

Nia makes a $600 car payment. About $470 goes to principal and about $130 goes to interest. She sends one extra $600 payment marked principal only, and the entire $600 comes off her balance. That single payment removes more than a full month from her loan, because every dollar avoided also avoids all the future interest it would have generated.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Ask any lender to apply extra payments directly to principal. Left unspecified, it often just counts toward next month, which changes nothing about what you owe.
