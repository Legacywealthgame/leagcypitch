What is the difference between a credit card and a debit card?

The Answer

A debit card takes money directly from your bank account; a credit card is borrowing money that you must pay back later.

The Fact

A debit card spends money you already have. A credit card creates a short-term loan every time it is used. The plastic looks nearly identical and the transaction feels identical at the register, which is precisely why the distinction gets lost.

Why This Matters

The difference shows up in three places most people never think about. Credit cards build payment history and a credit score, debit cards generally do not. Credit cards typically carry stronger fraud protection, since disputed charges are the bank's money until resolved rather than yours. And credit cards charge interest if not paid in full, while debit cards never can. Same swipe, entirely different consequences.

A Real Example

Two people both have $900 stolen through card fraud. Kendra used a credit card. She disputes the charge and the balance is removed while it is investigated. Her account was never actually short. Malik used a debit card. The $900 left his checking account immediately. He gets it back eventually, but in the meantime his rent payment bounces and he pays two overdraft fees for money that was never his fault.

Think Like a Wealth Builder

Use credit for the protection and the history. Pay it like a debit card so it never becomes a loan.
